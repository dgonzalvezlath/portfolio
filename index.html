Master's Thesis · Stockholm Business School · 2026

The MiFIR Single Volume Cap and Market Quality on Nasdaq Stockholm

Venue migration, dark trading, and displayed liquidity after the MiFIR Review

The thesis examines how a major EU equity-market regulation reshapes trading activity on Nasdaq Stockholm — asking whether restricting dark trading changes where order flow goes, and whether any of that migration translates into measurable improvements in displayed market quality.

Using the first SVC implementation wave (October 2025) as a quasi-natural experiment, the study applies a difference-in-differences design with entropy-balanced matched controls to high-frequency TAQ data. Outcomes include dark-trading share, periodic auction share, quoted bid-ask spreads, and market depth, with stock and date fixed effects. The findings point most clearly to changes in venue composition — dark trading decreases for treated stocks and periodic auction activity shows signs of increase — while displayed market-quality effects such as spreads and depth appear limited or statistically insignificant.

Research questionHow did the MiFIR Single Volume Cap affect trading venue composition and displayed market quality for Nasdaq Stockholm equities?
MotivationThe SVC, introduced as part of the MiFIR Review, restricts dark trading under the reference price waiver. The thesis asks whether this redirects order flow toward lit, periodic-auction, SI, or OTC venues — and whether that migration is followed by measurable improvements in displayed liquidity.
DataHigh-frequency trade-and-quote data (TAQ) around the October 2025 SVC implementation wave on Nasdaq Stockholm, covering lit, dark, periodic auction, SI, and OTC venues. Treated stocks are those affected by the suspension; matched controls are selected via entropy balancing.
MethodologyDifference-in-differences with entropy-balanced matched controls. Outcomes include dark trading share, periodic auction share, quoted bid-ask spread, and market depth. Models use stock and date fixed effects with standard errors clustered at the stock level.
Key findingsDark trading decreased for treated stocks following the SVC suspension. Periodic auction activity showed signs of increase, consistent with order-flow migration. Displayed market-quality effects — bid-ask spreads and depth — appear limited or statistically insignificant.
InterpretationThe regulation appears to affect where trading happens more clearly than it improves displayed liquidity. Dark-trading restrictions seem to redirect order flow rather than automatically translating into better execution quality on lit venues.
LimitationsResults are drawn from a short event window around a single SVC implementation wave. They should be read as evidence from one regulatory event rather than a general conclusion about dark-trading restrictions.
RelevanceThe thesis sits at the intersection of financial regulation, trading venue structure, post-trade infrastructure, and empirical finance — connecting directly to the operational work in securities services and to broader interest in European securities markets.
Illustrative venue migration around the SVC event. Dark trading exposure decreases for treated stocks, while displayed market-quality effects (spreads, depth) appear limited or statistically insignificant.

Liquidity Analysis of NIBE Industrier B on Nasdaq Stockholm

Analysed quoted and effective spreads across trading venue types using two weeks of TAQ data, and examined how intraday liquidity patterns shifted around a macroeconomic shock.

Representative effective spreads (bps) by venue type. OTC trades show the widest spreads; periodic auctions (PA) the narrowest.
Research questionHow does effective spread vary across trading venue types on Nasdaq Stockholm, and how did a macroeconomic shock affect intraday liquidity patterns?
DataTwo weeks of high-frequency trade-and-quote data for NIBE Industrier B, covering lit, dark, systematic internalisers (SI), OTC, and periodic auction venues.
MethodLiquidity measures including quoted and effective spreads and market depth. Data cleaned following Hagströmer & Landsberg (2024) conventions for continuous trading sessions.
Key resultEffective spreads were widest for OTC trades and narrowest for periodic auctions. During the Liberation Day tariff event window, lit-venue spreads widened and dark/SI volumes declined — though isolating a single cause requires broader controls.
ToolsR, Excel
What I learnedVenue fragmentation creates materially different liquidity profiles for the same stock; understanding these differences is foundational for post-trade analysis and securities operations.

Private Placement Discounts & Abnormal Returns

Event study of PIPE transactions on Swedish listed firms. Measured cumulative abnormal returns around announcement dates and tested information asymmetry predictions following Myers & Majluf (1984).

Average CAR around PIPE announcement date. Negative abnormal returns accumulate sharply at announcement and persist post-event.
Research questionHow do markets react to PIPE (private placement) announcements on Swedish listed companies, and is this consistent with the Myers & Majluf information asymmetry hypothesis?
DataPIPE transactions by Swedish listed firms; daily stock prices and market index returns from Nasdaq Stockholm.
MethodEvent study with market model estimation over a 250-day estimation window; cumulative abnormal returns (CARs) computed over the [−10, +10] event window; cross-sectional OLS regression testing information asymmetry.
Key resultAnnouncements were associated with negative CARs in the event window, broadly consistent with adverse selection — markets appear to interpret private placements as a signal that insiders believe the stock is overvalued. Results align with the existing PIPE literature for small-cap markets.
ToolsExcel, R
What I learnedInformation asymmetry has measurable and persistent price effects; estimation and event windows materially affect CAR estimates and inference.
Event Study Abnormal Returns Information Asymmetry Swedish Equities

Government Bond Yield Co-movement: Sweden, US & Germany

Analysed 20 years of weekly 5-year government bond yield data across three jurisdictions. Applied ADF unit-root tests and linked major rate movements to central-bank policy events.

5-year government bond yields 2004–2024. All three series declined toward zero through the mid-2010s before the 2022 rate-hike cycle.
Research questionHow do 5-year government bond yields co-move across Sweden, the United States, and Germany over a 20-year period, and what drives the largest single-week movements?
DataWeekly 5-year government bond yield data from the Riksbank for Sweden, the United States, and Germany (2004–2024).
MethodVisual time-series analysis; ADF unit-root tests with and without trend; identification of outlier weekly yield changes; qualitative linkage to central-bank policy events (ECB, Fed, Riksbank).
Key resultAll three series were non-stationary in levels. The largest weekly moves in Swedish 5-year yields broadly aligned with ECB and Federal Reserve announcement dates — suggesting strong international monetary policy transmission that often dominates domestic Riksbank decisions in market impact.
ToolsR, Excel
What I learnedSmall open economies are highly exposed to international interest rate cycles; this matters directly for fixed income pricing, bank ALM, and the valuation of rate-sensitive assets in post-trade securities operations.
ADF Unit Root Test Government Bonds Monetary Policy Riksbank Data

Bank Balance Sheet Risk & Duration Gap Analysis

Applied gap analysis and duration gap concepts to a bank's financial statements to assess interest-rate sensitivity. Evaluated a futures-based hedge and reviewed Basel III capital requirements.

Estimated NII change under parallel rate shifts. A positive duration gap creates large downside under rate rises; a futures overlay substantially reduces this sensitivity.
Research questionHow exposed is a bank's net interest income to parallel interest rate shifts given a positive duration gap, and how effectively can a futures overlay reduce this sensitivity?
DataBank financial statement data from course material; interest rate sensitivity assumptions; futures pricing based on prevailing market rates.
MethodGAP analysis; duration gap calculation from asset and liability durations; NII simulation under ±100bp and ±200bp parallel rate shocks; hedging effectiveness assessed by overlaying interest-rate futures to target a near-zero duration gap.
Key resultA positive duration gap created meaningful downside NII exposure under rate-rising scenarios. The futures overlay substantially reduced this sensitivity across all shock scenarios, illustrating the practical trade-off between hedging cost and risk reduction.
ToolsExcel
What I learnedAsset-liability mismatches create non-trivial interest rate risk even for well-capitalised banks; this connects directly to Basel III IRRBB requirements and the risk-management frameworks that govern banking institutions.
Asset-Liability Management Duration Gap Interest Rate Risk Basel III
Advanced Financial Empirical Research

GARCH Volatility Modelling in Financial Return Series

Estimated and compared GARCH(1,1) and EGARCH specifications on equity return data to study volatility clustering and asymmetric leverage effects in Stata.

Volatility clustering: calm periods alternate with high-volatility clusters, with realised volatility rising sharply when returns are large.
Research questionWhich model — GARCH(1,1) or EGARCH — better captures volatility dynamics in equity return data, and is there evidence of an asymmetric leverage effect?
DataMonthly equity return series sourced from Yahoo Finance, using 81 months for training and 4 months as a hold-out test set.
MethodMaximum likelihood estimation of GARCH and EGARCH specifications; model comparison via AIC/BIC; Ljung-Box tests for residual autocorrelation; out-of-sample RMSE comparison.
Key resultEGARCH provided a better in-sample fit, capturing the leverage effect where negative shocks produce larger volatility increases than positive shocks of equal magnitude. All four models overshot realised prices in the test window, reflecting high volatility in the underlying series.
ToolsStata, R
What I learnedSymmetric volatility models systematically miss important dynamics in financial return series; model specification and data frequency both matter significantly for risk measurement.
GARCH / EGARCH Volatility Clustering Financial Time Series Stata

Cancel Culture & Stock Market Reactions

Event study on the stock-price impact of corporate reputational shocks (2011–2021). Tested whether firm size and a public apology moderated the market's reaction via cross-sectional OLS regression on CARs.

Average CAR by apology status across event sub-windows. Firms issuing a public apology showed smaller negative abnormal returns following reputational shock events.
Research questionDo corporate reputational shock events produce statistically significant abnormal stock returns, and does issuing a public apology or firm size moderate this effect?
DataCorporate reputational shock events (2011–2021) identified from media coverage; daily stock returns and market index returns.
MethodEvent study with market model; CARs computed over the [−10, +10] event window; cross-sectional OLS regression testing apology issuance and firm size as moderators of the market reaction.
Key resultNegative CARs were observed on average around announcement. Firms that issued public apologies showed smaller negative abnormal returns, suggesting markets partially reward transparent crisis communication. Firm size alone was not a statistically significant moderator.
ToolsR, Excel
What I learnedReputational events have quantifiable financial consequences; corporate communication choices are not merely symbolic — they appear to produce measurable market effects.
Event Study Reputational Risk CARs Cross-sectional OLS

Seminar project covering beta estimation, WACC-based optimal capital structure across ten debt/equity ratios, dividend policy, ownership structure, and full DCF valuation alongside relative valuation of 17 companies using P/E, P/S and P/BV.

Firm value peaks at 60% equity / 40% debt. WACC rises sharply as leverage pushes the credit rating below investment-grade thresholds.
Research questionWhat is Assa Abloy's optimal capital structure, and how does this compare with a DCF-based and relative valuation of the firm?
DataAssa Abloy annual report (2021); Yahoo Finance for 5-year beta regression; comparable company levered betas and debt ratios; analyst estimates for the pharmaceutical relative valuation exercise.
MethodBottom-up and regression beta estimation; WACC optimisation across 10 debt/equity ratios using a bond-rating framework; DCF with projected free cash flows and CAPM cost of equity; P/E, P/S, P/BV relative valuation of 17 pharmaceutical companies.
Key resultThe 60/40 equity-debt structure minimised WACC and maximised estimated firm value in the model. DCF produced a per-share estimate of 343.51 SEK, highly sensitive to growth and discount-rate assumptions. Only Astra appeared undervalued across all three price ratios in the pharmaceutical screen.

PayPal's Acquisition of iZettle — Cross-Border M&A & FX Risk

Case study of PayPal's $2.2bn acquisition of Swedish fintech iZettle. Covered M&A rationale, FX risk in extended international transactions, competition law across jurisdictions, and multi-method valuation against Adyen and Square.

P/S ratio comparison at time of acquisition. PayPal paid 23.4× sales — above Square but below Adyen, suggesting the price was within a defensible range given iZettle's European growth profile.
Research questionWas PayPal's $2.2 billion acquisition of iZettle strategically justified, and how does the acquisition price compare against valuation benchmarks?
DataPayPal and iZettle annual reports; Adyen IPO prospectus; Square annual report; Riksbank 10-year bond yield; market risk premium estimates; industry growth projections.
MethodPorter's Five Forces and Business Model Canvas for competitive context; relative valuation using P/S and P/Adj EBITDA against Square and Adyen; DCF with WACC estimated from comparable-company betas and the Riksbank risk-free rate.
Key resultThe acquisition P/S of 23.4× was above Square (8.6×) but below Adyen (31.7×), suggesting the price was defensible under optimistic growth assumptions. The DCF estimated firm value above the acquisition price, but with high uncertainty. Currency risk over the prolonged regulatory review was identified as a material and underappreciated deal risk.
ToolsExcel
What I learnedCross-border M&A valuation is highly sensitive to growth and discount-rate assumptions; regulatory review timelines and FX exposure can materially affect deal economics.
